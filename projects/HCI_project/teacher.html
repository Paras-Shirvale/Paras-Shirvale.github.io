What do these columns mean?
Mix-ups
How many times the child swapped, dropped or added a letter from this pair, reading or writing.
Per 100 words
Mix-ups divided by how many words containing those letters the child actually met, times 100. A hard passage has more of those words, so a raw count would look worse than it is; this removes that. Lower is better.
Caught it themselves
The share of mix-ups the child corrected within 2 seconds without being helped. Rising before the mix-up rate falls is the sign a skill is becoming automatic.
Trend
The average mix-ups per 100 words in the first half of the chapters against the second half. Improving: the second half is at least 25% lower. Rising: at least 25% higher. Steady: about the same. Needs at least three chapters, otherwise "not enough data". It is a signal, not a test result.
Help needed
The child's own use of the help tools, from lightest to most: prompt a nudge to look again, such as the word split into chunks; cue a clue to work it out, such as a picture, or saying it themselves; choice picking between two or three options; supply being told the answer, such as hearing the word read. Mostly blue means a little help was enough; mostly pink means the answer had to be given.

Mix-ups per 100 words, over time

One line per letter pair. Falling lines mean that pair is getting easier. Each point is one chapter (reading and writing together), adjusted for how many words with those letters it contained.

Catching their own slips

Share of mix-ups fixed within 2 seconds. Rising before errors fall is a sign the skill is becoming automatic.

Hesitation on target words

Average time per word: words with each letter pair, against all other words.

How much help was needed

Cue cards per session, by rung. Fewer, and lower rungs, means more independent.

Which kind of help works

How often each kind of cue got the child moving again, and how often they said "Not now".

Reading aloud vs writing

Mix-ups per pair in each mode. More in writing than reading points to spelling memory, not decoding.

What kind of mistakes

Swapped letters, letters left out, wrong order, and so on.
